# Interview Checklist

## Project

**Fintech Digital Banking QA Lab**

---

# Overview

This checklist helps prepare for QA Engineer, API Test Engineer, Automation QA Engineer, SDET, and fintech QA interviews.

It covers:

* CV preparation
* LinkedIn and GitHub
* Portfolio readiness
* Company research
* Technical preparation
* Behavioural preparation
* Interview-day setup
* Questions for interviewers
* Post-interview follow-up

The goal is to reduce avoidable mistakes and ensure that both technical ability and professional communication are presented clearly.

---

# How to Use This Checklist

Complete the checklist in stages rather than waiting until the day of the interview.

Recommended timing:

| Period           | Focus                              |
| ---------------- | ---------------------------------- |
| 1–2 weeks before | Research and technical preparation |
| 3–5 days before  | Portfolio and answer practice      |
| 1 day before     | Final checks and environment setup |
| Interview day    | Calm review and execution          |
| After interview  | Reflection and follow-up           |

## When You Do Not Know the Answer

Use an approach such as:

> I have not implemented that directly yet, so I would not want to overstate my experience. My current understanding is that it works by ____. # Section 27 — Live Testing Exercise

If asked to test a feature live:

## Suggested Approach

1. Clarify the feature.
2. Identify users and goals.
3. Identify business risks.
4. Test the happy path.
5. Test negative cases.
6. Test boundaries.
7. Test security and permissions.
8. Check API or network behaviour.
9. Consider data persistence.
10. Summarise findings.

---

## Speak Your Reasoning

Example:

> I am starting with the successful flow to understand the expected behaviour. After that, I will test validation, permissions, boundaries, duplicate submission, and recovery because those areas usually contain higher-risk defects.

# QA Lead Conclusion

Interview preparation should combine technical knowledge, practical evidence, honest communication, and professional organisation.

A strong QA candidate should be ready to explain:

* What they tested
* Why they tested it
* Which risks they identified
* How they investigated failures
* How they validated APIs and data
* How they supported release decisions
* How they would improve quality over time

Use this checklist before every interview, update it based on experience, and treat each interview as another opportunity to improve.
